# Project 02 Reflection

When I started this portfolio, I was worried that building another personal website would feel too close to the HTML and CSS portfolio I had made before. I decided the new site needed a clearer audience and a better reason to exist and a lot more technical in a way. I focused it on cybersecurity and intelligence analysis because those fields connect to my Army Reserve training and future corrections intelligence unit work I want to pursue. That decision changed what belonged on the site that is well polished and be used to show future employers.

The category filter is one part I am happy with as it solves a real layout problem being all weird and unorganized as the security plans, intelligence work, and coursework do not all have to compete for space at once. I also like the saved theme control, but the biggest improvement after P01 was the 5K Preparation Knowledge Base case study. A link to the finished site did not tell anyone why I organized it the way I did. Writing about the problem, process, and decisions made the work easier for another person to evaluate. Adding my photograph also made the portfolio feel more personal than the large initials I used earlier.

The hardest decision was how to handle the cybersecurity projects. My iteration plan called for one completed technical lab with evidence and screenshots. I have not finished that lab, and I did not want a polished card to suggest I had. I kept four projects marked Coming Soon and made a separate plans page explaining what I would test and what evidence I would publish. That page gives the ideas more substance, but it is not a substitute for completing the labs. An employer can review my completed coursework now the security and intelligence projects still need real results before they become portfolio case studies but this portfolio remains as a space where I can come back and continue updating. 

If I started over, I would build the first technical project much earlier. I spent a lot of time changing the visual direction before I had enough finished work to show. I would also decide on a small set of reusable layout patterns and test the phone view from the beginning. That would have saved time when I added the case study and the new plans page. I would keep the plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript approach, though. For this size of project, being able to open a file and understand how it works is useful.

I learned that a portfolio is partly a navigation problem and partly an honesty problem. Clear labels help people find the right work, and a case study helps them see how I think. But the labels also need to tell the truth about what is complete. I learned to check the live version after publishing, because a working local page does not prove that its links, files, and images are available on GitHub Pages. The next step is to complete the Wireshark DNS lab, document its evidence, and replace its plan with a case study that shows what I actually found and show this to future employers.
